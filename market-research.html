FLAGSHIP RESEARCH PROJECT

AI-Assisted Market Decision Research Platform

A long-running independent engineering and research program exploring AI-assisted experimentation, Python simulation, sequential decision systems, and rigorous evaluation using real-world market data.

The specific market instruments, production decision logic, parameters, and proprietary research findings have intentionally been abstracted. This case study focuses on the engineering, experimentation, and evaluation systems developed around the research.

The Challenge

Researching decision systems in noisy real-world environments creates several engineering challenges. Strong apparent results can be caused by overfitting, data leakage, favorable outliers, inconsistent execution assumptions, or accidental changes in experimental methodology.

I developed a structured research environment designed to turn ambiguous ideas into reproducible experiments while maintaining strict separation between discovery and evaluation.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Problems I Had to Solve

Building the research system required solving problems that extended beyond model or policy performance. Experimental integrity, reproducibility, robustness, and information control became core engineering requirements.

Preventing Data Leakage

Designed strict separation between discovery and evaluation environments so later observations could not influence previously developed candidate systems.

Reproducible Experiments

Standardized experiment rules, inputs, execution assumptions, metrics, and versioned configurations so results could be independently replayed and compared.

Outlier Dependence

Developed stress tests that remove unusually favorable outcomes while preserving adverse outcomes to identify systems whose apparent success depends on rare events.

AI Research Governance

Defined explicit constraints around what information AI-assisted workflows could access, how experiments could be modified, and when candidate configurations had to be frozen.

Consistent Simulation

Built deterministic execution rules so timing, resource limits, costs, and outcome calculations remained consistent across large numbers of experiments.

Research Handoffs

Created structured documentation, run summaries, configuration packages, and implementation notes so experiments could be resumed and audited without relying on informal context.

EXPERIMENTAL PROCESS

From Idea to Frozen Evaluation

Candidate systems move through a controlled research lifecycle. Development is separated from later evaluation stages so that validation results cannot be used to retroactively improve the system being tested.

01 Research Question

Define the objective, constraints, and evaluation criteria.

→
02 Discovery

Develop and test candidate approaches in the permitted research environment.

→
03 Freeze

Lock the candidate rules before exposure to evaluation data.

→
04 Validation

Replay the frozen system against previously unseen observations.

→
05 Stress Test

Evaluate robustness under deliberately adverse assumptions.

→
06 Confirmation

Perform final evaluation without modifying the frozen system.

Key Lessons

The project increasingly shifted from simply searching for strong results toward designing systems capable of distinguishing robust behavior from misleading experimental success.

This required treating evaluation methodology, reproducibility, and information isolation as core engineering problems rather than secondary analysis tasks.
